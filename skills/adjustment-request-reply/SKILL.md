# Adjustment Request Reply

Produces the reply to a candidate who has asked for something to change about their interview or assessment: thanks, exactly what will change, what else might help, who will know and a way to talk anything through. Plus a two-line note for the panel. Or checks a reply someone has drafted. A quick, specific answer lets the candidate prepare, and keeps the request from becoming the reason they drop out.

## When to use

- "A candidate has asked for extra time and the questions in writing. Help me reply."
- "Someone's asked if there's another way to do the group exercise. What do I say?"
- "Check this reply to an adjustment request before I send it."
- "Can I ask what their condition is so I know what to put in place?"
- "How much do I tell the panel about a candidate's adjustment?"

Wrong tool for the adjustments line in the invitation (use Interview Invitation) or for sharing questions with every candidate (use Advance Questions Sheet). Adjustments to the job itself after an offer belong to your onboarding and occupational health process.

## Inputs

Ask once for anything missing. Defaults in brackets.

1. **Essential:** what the candidate has asked for, in their words, with any diagnosis or health detail taken out first. [paste the request with the condition removed]
2. **Essential:** the interview or assessment: format, length, each part (questions, group exercise, role-play, task, presentation), date and panel roles. [one structured video interview, 45 minutes, two panel members]
3. What you can already agree, and anything you're unsure about. [everything asked for is agreed unless you say otherwise]
4. What each part of the interview assesses, so any alternative still tests the same criterion. [taken from an attached interview plan or scoring guide]
5. Who has to know to set the adjustment up. [the panel chair, and whoever books the room or sends the link]
6. Who replies, and how the candidate can reach them. [the recruitment coordinator, by email or phone]

Never ask for the candidate's name, diagnosis, condition, medication or medical evidence. If the user pastes a request that names a condition, work from what the candidate asked for, keep the condition out of the reply and the panel note, and tell the user you've done so.

## Steps

Numbers in square brackets in these steps and the Quality checks are defaults. Use the user's own figure if they give one; otherwise use the default without asking.

1. Read what the user has given, including the candidate's message, the invitation and any interview plan. If they've given an existing reply, this is a check job: run the Quality checks on it, list every problem with where it sits and why it matters, then write a corrected reply and panel note.
2. Ask for missing essentials in one message, five questions at most, showing the defaults. If they say "just draft it", use the defaults and list the assumptions.
3. Start from what the candidate has asked for and what they find hard. Section 20 of the Equality Act 2010 requires reasonable adjustments where something about the process (the format, a group exercise, a time limit) puts a disabled candidate at a substantial disadvantage. Agree a sensible change on the candidate's word. Section 60 allows health questions before a job offer only for set purposes, and making adjustments to the process is one, so ask only what you need to put the change in place ("Would the questions on paper on the day help, or would you like them a few days ahead too?"). Never ask what the condition is, and never ask for a GP letter, a diagnosis or other medical evidence.
4. Sort each request into one of three. Agree it as asked. Agree a version of it, and say what and why. Or talk it through first, because as asked it would stop the panel comparing candidates on the same criteria, for example dropping a part that tests a criterion everyone is scored on. For the third, the reply never says no. It explains in a line what that part assesses, suggests one or two other ways to test the same thing (a one-to-one version of a group exercise with an assessor, a written answer in place of a spoken one, a task split in two with a break) and offers a call at a set time. An EAT judgment reported by Lewis Silkin in October 2026 noted that adjustments that stop an employer comparing candidates fairly are unlikely to be reasonable. Treat that as a reason to look for another way to assess the same thing, and tell the user to read the judgment before relying on it.
5. Offer a short list of other adjustments that fit this interview, so the candidate can pick: extra time for answers or tasks; questions in writing on the day or in advance; breaks; a quieter room, or cameras off for part of a video call; a different format (video, phone or in person); a different time of day; captions, a BSL interpreter or a support worker; a screen reader or other assistive tool. Leave out any that don't fit.
6. Decide who will be told and how little. The panel chair and anyone who has to set the change up (the room booker, the person sending the link) learn what will change, for example "Candidate B has the questions on paper and 10 extra minutes". They never learn why, and the candidate's own message stays with the person handling the request. Keep the record with the recruitment file and delete it under your retention policy.
7. Write the reply: thank them for telling you; confirm each agreed change in concrete terms (what, when, who sets it up); ask what else would help; name any part you'd like to talk through and offer a time; say who will be told and that they'll be told only what's changing; say they'll be assessed on the same criteria as everyone else and the adjustment won't count against them; give a contact. Keep it warm and short, under [250] words, in "you" and "we". Send it within [two] working days of the request and in time to set things up.
8. Write the panel note separately: the candidate letter, the date and time, what changes, what the panel does differently, and a reminder to score on the same criteria and anchors as every candidate.
9. Run the Quality checks and fix what fails.
10. Present the reply and the panel note, then **Assumptions** and **Check before you use it**: that each agreed change can be set up in time, the date and panel, who will be told, and your retention period.
11. Offer a .docx, a short phone script for talking an adjustment through, or the Advance Questions Sheet skill if questions in advance would help every candidate.

## Output format

**Reply to the candidate**

**Subject:** Your [interview / assessment] for [role] on [weekday date]: what we'll change

Hi [first name],

Thank you for letting us know what would help. [One line that answers their message directly.]

**What we'll change:**
- [Adjustment]: [exactly what, when, and who sets it up]

**Anything else that would help?** [One question about what would help, e.g. "Would a short break between the two parts be useful?"] [Optional list from step 5]

**Something we'd like to talk through:** [only if needed: the part, what it assesses, one or two alternatives, a time to call]

**Who will know:** [roles] will be told what's changing so they can set it up. They won't be told why, and your message stays with me.

You'll be assessed on the same criteria as everyone else, and the change won't count against you. Any questions, contact [role] at [contact].

[Sender name], [role]

**Panel note** (for the chair and anyone setting it up)

Candidate [letter], [weekday date, time]: [what changes]. [What the panel does differently.] Score against the same criteria and anchors as every candidate.

The reply under [250] words; the panel note under 60.

## Quality checks

- The reply asks for no diagnosis, condition, medical evidence or GP letter, and every question in it is about what would help.
- Every agreed adjustment is confirmed in concrete terms: what changes, when, and who sets it up. Nothing is left as "we'll try" or "on the day".
- Nothing the candidate asked for is refused in the reply. Anything not agreed as asked comes with what that part assesses, at least one alternative and an offer to talk at a set time.
- The reply says who will be told, and the panel note gives only the candidate letter and what changes, never the reason, a condition or the candidate's own words.
- The reply goes out within [two] working days of the request and leaves time to set the change up before the interview.
- The reply says the candidate will be assessed on the same criteria and the adjustment won't count against them.

## Guardrails

- The one rule for this pack: Claude drafts the questions, guides and letters. Interviewers gather the evidence, and people make every hiring decision. This reply sets up a fair interview for one candidate. The panel gathers the evidence on the same criteria as everyone else, and people decide.
- Personal data: adjustment requests often reveal health information, which UK GDPR and the Data Protection Act 2018 treat as special category data. Take the condition out before pasting a request into Claude, share only what changes and only with the people who set it up, and delete the record under your retention policy. The candidate can ask for what you hold about them, including notes about the adjustment. Follow your organisation's AI policy.
- Fairness: handle every request on the same basis, whoever makes it. An adjustment changes how a candidate shows their evidence, and the criteria and the scoring stay the same for everyone. Never infer or mention protected characteristics, and keep the request out of anything the panel scores.
- Law: section 20 of the Equality Act 2010 (reasonable adjustments), section 60 (health questions before an offer, which allows questions needed to make adjustments to the process) and the EHRC Employment Code. Failing to make a reasonable adjustment is a form of discrimination under the Act, and so is treating a candidate less well because of their disability. Check the current versions. This is a draft, never legal advice; take advice where you think an adjustment may not be reasonable.
- No invented facts: what can be agreed, dates, times and contacts come from the user or stay as placeholders. Never promise an adjustment the user hasn't confirmed can be set up.
