# Advance Questions Sheet

Produces the sheet candidates get before their interview, with the main questions or the themes you'll cover, and a sending plan that gets it to every candidate the same way and the same time ahead. Or checks a sheet and plan someone already has. Every candidate gets time to think about their examples, and nobody has to disclose anything or ask for an adjustment to get it.

## When to use

- "We want to start sending interview questions out in advance. What should we share?"
- "Draft the advance questions sheet for the team leader interviews."
- "Should we send the actual questions or just the topics?"
- "Check this sheet and the way we're planning to send it."
- "A candidate asked for the questions beforehand. Should everyone get them?"

Wrong tool for writing the questions themselves (use Structured Interview Guide) or the rating guide (use Scoring Guide). When one candidate asks for the questions ahead as an adjustment, reply with Adjustment Request Reply, then consider sending this sheet to everyone.

## Inputs

Ask once for anything missing. Defaults in brackets.

1. **Essential:** the main questions from your interview guide for one role and stage, or the criteria they test. [taken from an attached Structured Interview Guide]
2. **Essential:** the interview dates and the day invitations go out. [placeholders]
3. Whether to share the main questions word for word or the themes. [the main questions]
4. How it goes out and who sends it. [the recruitment coordinator, by email from the ATS, with the invitation]
5. Whether any candidates are internal, and whether any part is a live task or a situational question that tests thinking on the spot. [a mix of internal and external candidates; no live task]
6. Whether candidates may bring notes into the interview. [yes]

Never ask for candidates' names, which candidates have asked for adjustments, or why.

## Steps

Numbers in square brackets in these steps and the Quality checks are defaults. Use the user's own figure if they give one; otherwise use the default without asking.

1. Read what the user has given, including any interview guide, scoring guide or invitation they attach. If they've given an existing sheet or sending plan, this is a check job: run the Quality checks on both, list every problem with where it sits and why it matters, then write a corrected sheet and plan.
2. Ask for missing essentials in one message, five questions at most, showing the defaults. If they say "just draft it", use the defaults, leave any missing question as [question from the guide], and list the assumptions.
3. Decide what to share and give the reason in one line. The main questions word for word give the most preparation and suit questions about past experience. Themes ("a time you helped someone in your team improve") suit roles where the panel needs to see some thinking on the spot. Either way, keep these for the day: the probes under each question, any situational follow-ups, and any question that tests how someone reacts in the moment.
4. Set the timing. Send it [a week] before each interview, the gap Autistica suggests. The National Autistic Society suggests a few days, so never send it less than [three] working days ahead. Pick one fixed point for everyone, such as with the invitation or seven days before each interview. If someone is booked at short notice, send it with the invitation and offer a later slot if they'd like more time.
5. Check who gets it. The same sheet goes to every candidate invited to the stage, by the same route at the same point: internal and external candidates, agency candidates, and people who asked for adjustments as well as people who didn't. If internal candidates could see it earlier, on an intranet page or a Teams channel, publish it for everyone at the same moment or keep it off internal channels. If the user wants to share only on request, explain the trade-off. Sharing questions with one candidate as an adjustment is lawful and often right. A sheet that some candidates get and others don't gives the ones who get it more time to prepare, and makes everyone else disclose a need to get the same.
6. Write the sheet. Open with what it is and that every candidate at this stage gets the same one. Then the interview's format: length, the panel by role, the number of main questions, and that there'll be follow-up questions on the day. Then the questions or themes in the order they'll be asked, each with the criterion it relates to in a few plain words. Write each so someone from outside the organisation understands it: replace or explain internal system names, scorecards, team names and acronyms. Add a line on preparing: a real example works best (the situation, what you did and what happened, which is the STAR shape), and notes are welcome if allowed.
7. Strip out everything from the scoring guide: rating anchors, scores, "a strong answer will mention", lists of what the panel will score highly. The criterion's name and a one-line description, as in the job description, are enough. The rating guide stays with the panel, so the interview still tells candidates apart on the evidence they give.
8. Add the adjustments line (anything else that would help, a contact and a date, no reason needed) and a contact for questions.
9. Write the panel note: candidates have seen the main questions; reading from notes is fine; use the probes to get to the detail; score the evidence in each answer, and never mark an answer down for being prepared.
10. If the user or their hiring managers ask who else does this, give them the evidence plainly. John Lewis published its interview questions in 2024. Vodafone UK shares questions ahead and keeps role-specific ones for the day. West Midlands Employers treat sharing questions as one adjustment that can give neurodivergent candidates parity. An Employment Appeal Tribunal judgment reported by Lewis Silkin in October 2026 upheld a finding that reasonable adjustments were made where all candidates were offered the questions in advance, and noted that adjustments that stop an employer comparing candidates fairly are unlikely to be reasonable. Tell the user to read the judgment itself before relying on it, since this is one case as a law firm reported it.
11. Run the Quality checks and fix what fails.
12. Present the sending plan and the sheet, then **Assumptions** and **Check before you use it**: the questions match the final interview guide in wording and order, the dates, the sending route, that no internal channel already holds a copy, and that the panel has the panel note.
13. Offer a .docx, a themes version or a full-questions version, or the Interviewer Briefing skill so the panel knows what candidates have seen.

## Output format

**Sending plan: [role], [stage]**

| Who gets it | Route | When | Sent by |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Every candidate invited to [stage], internal and external | [email from the ATS] | [with the invitation, or seven days before each interview; never less than three working days] | [role] |

**Panel note:** [two lines for the interviewers: candidates have the main questions; notes are allowed; probes and follow-ups stay for the day; score the evidence in each answer]

**The sheet**

**Your interview for [role]: the questions we'll ask** [or: the themes we'll cover]

[Two sentences: why you're getting this, and that every candidate at this stage gets the same sheet.]

**The interview:** [length], [video or in person], with [panel roles]. We'll ask [number] main questions in this order, with follow-up questions on the day.

1. [Question or theme in plain words] ([criterion]: [one-line description])
2. [Question or theme] ([criterion]: [one-line description])

**Preparing:** Answers work best as a real example: the situation, what you did and what happened. [You're welcome to bring notes.]

**Anything else that would help?** Tell [role] by [date] at [contact]. You don't need to say why.

**Questions:** [contact role and contact]

One page for the sheet.

## Quality checks

- The sending plan gives the same sheet to every candidate invited to the stage, internal and external, by the same route at the same point, and no internal channel gets it earlier.
- The sheet reaches every candidate at least [three] working days before their interview, aiming for [a week].
- The sheet holds no rating anchors, scores, scoring indicators or "what a strong answer includes", and none of the probes or situational follow-ups kept for the day.
- Every question or theme makes sense to a candidate from outside the organisation, with no unexplained acronym, system name, scorecard or team nickname.
- The questions or themes match the interview guide in content and order, and the sheet says there will be follow-up questions on the day.
- The panel note tells interviewers that candidates have the main questions and that notes are allowed.
- No candidate names, adjustment details or health information appear.

## Guardrails

- The one rule for this pack: Claude drafts the questions, guides and letters. Interviewers gather the evidence, and people make every hiring decision. This sheet gives every candidate the same preparation. The panel still gathers the evidence through the answers and the follow-ups, and people decide.
- Personal data: the sheet is the same for everyone, so it carries no candidate data. Never paste in who asked for adjustments or why. Follow your organisation's AI policy.
- Fairness: the same sheet, the same route and the same time ahead for everyone at the stage. Check the wording for insider language that favours internal candidates, and for anything that assumes a background ("in your last office role"). Never infer or mention protected characteristics.
- Law: a sheet for everyone can mean fewer individual requests, and the duty under section 20 of the Equality Act 2010 to make reasonable adjustments still applies to any disabled candidate who needs more, such as extra time or the questions in writing on the day. The EHRC Employment Code covers recruitment, including interviews. The October 2026 EAT report is one case; read the judgment before relying on it. Check the current versions. This is a draft, never legal advice.
- No invented facts: questions, dates and sending routes come from the user or the interview guide, or stay as placeholders. Never write a new question to fill a gap.
