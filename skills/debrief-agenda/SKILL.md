# Debrief Agenda

Gives the chair of a hiring panel's debrief a timed agenda and ground rules: scores in before anyone talks, one criterion at a time, evidence against the scoring guide, and the decision written down with its reasons before people leave. Or checks a debrief process someone already uses. It stops the wash-up drifting into who the hiring manager liked, and leaves a record the panel can stand behind.

## When to use

- "Interviews for the team leader roles finish on Wednesday. How should we run the wash-up?"
- "Our debriefs turn into the hiring manager saying who they liked. Give me a structure."
- "Write an agenda for Thursday's panel debrief: three candidates, four criteria, 45 minutes."
- "Check our debrief template before the next round."
- "How do we stop the director's view swinging the whole panel?"

Wrong tool for scoring answers or choosing who to hire, which the panel does. To write the decision up afterwards, use Decision Record; to set the rating anchors, use Scoring Guide.

## Inputs

Ask once for anything missing. Defaults in brackets.

1. **Essential:** the criteria in order, the scoring scale and the anchors. [the criteria and 1 to 4 scale from your Scoring Guide]
2. **Essential:** the panel by role, from most junior to most senior, and who chairs. [the TA partner chairs; a chair who also interviewed scores like everyone else and speaks last; panel members speak from most junior to most senior]
3. How many candidates, as letters, and how many vacancies. [three candidates, one vacancy]
4. Length, format and timing of the meeting. [60 minutes by video, within two working days of the last interview]
5. How scores reach the chair and when they lock. [through the ATS scorecard, submitted and locked before the meeting]
6. The decision options and any rules agreed before interviews, such as a minimum score on essential criteria, and whether you have a positive action policy. [appoint, reserve list or not appointed; at least 3 on every essential criterion; no positive action policy]

Never ask for candidates' names, health, the reasons behind an adjustment, or protected characteristics. If the user includes them, use letters and roles instead and say you've done so.

## Steps

1. Read what the user has given, including any debrief template, scoring guide or past notes. If they've given an existing agenda or process, this is a check job: run the Quality checks on it, list every problem with where it sits and why it matters, then write a corrected agenda.
2. Ask for missing essentials once, in one message, five questions at most, with the defaults shown. If the user says "just draft it", use the defaults and list them as assumptions.
3. Set the rules for before the meeting. Each panel member scores alone, straight after each interview, against the scoring guide, and writes the evidence for each score: what the candidate said or did. Everyone submits before the debrief, and nobody discusses candidates between interviews. This is the evaluation side of interview structure described by Campion, Palmer and Campion (1997): each answer rated on an anchored scale, notes taken, several interviewers, no discussion between interviews. The chair confirms every score is in before the meeting starts. A panel member whose scores aren't in doesn't speak on that candidate until they've submitted.
4. Order the agenda criterion by criterion, in the scoring guide's order, taking every candidate on one criterion before moving to the next. Keeping each criterion separate limits halo, where one strong answer colours the rest. For a single candidate, keep the same order.
5. Set the speaking order for every criterion: the most junior panel member first, then upwards, with the hiring manager and the most senior member last. The first view in the room then comes from the person least likely to anchor everyone else. Each gives their score and the evidence behind it, and nobody comments on another's score until all have spoken.
6. Write how disagreement is handled. Where scores differ by two points or more, the panel goes back to the evidence and the anchors only. Anyone may change a score after hearing the evidence, and the chair records the change and the reason. Only evidence in someone's notes from the interview counts.
7. Write the ground rules the chair reads out, and redirects for when talk leaves the evidence ("What did they say or do that shows that?", "Which anchor does that match?"). The chair stops:
   - impressions with no evidence behind them, and any "overall feel" or "gut feel";
   - "culture fit" (ask which criterion they mean, and score against that);
   - comparisons with the person who held the job before;
   - anything about age, family, health, accent, where someone is from or any other protected characteristic;
   - for internal candidates, knowledge from outside the interview, unless the same kind of evidence was gathered for every candidate.
8. Add the bias watch, one line each on what it sounds like and what the chair says: halo and its reverse, horns; similarity (rating up people like us); first impressions (the opening minutes outweighing the rest); contrast (judging a candidate against the one seen just before).
9. Write the decision item. Once every criterion is done, the panel applies the decision rules agreed before interviews to its scores and makes its decision on each candidate. Where more candidates meet the rules than there are vacancies, the panel goes back through the criteria in order, comparing the evidence, and records which criteria decided it; Claude never orders the candidates. The named recorder writes down, on the day: the decision, the reasons by criterion, any panel member's dissent, who was present and the date. A view across criteria comes from the scores and the rules, so there's no separate "overall feel" round. If the organisation has a positive action policy and the panel finds two candidates as qualified as each other on the evidence, the chair pauses and takes HR advice on the section 159 conditions before the tie-break is used. It's never automatic, and Claude never raises it.
10. Close with actions: who tells each candidate and by when, who drafts feedback, references only after an offer and with consent, and where the notes are kept.
11. Set timings so the items add up to the meeting length, with more time on criteria where the user expects scores to differ.
12. If the user pastes the submitted scores, lay them out by criterion and mark where panel members differ by two points or more. Leave out totals, rankings and any comment on a candidate.
13. Run the Quality checks and fix what fails before showing anything.
14. Present the agenda, then **Assumptions** and **Check before you use it**: the panel's seniority order, the decision rules agreed before interviews, who records the decision, and your retention period for debrief notes.
15. Offer a .docx, a one-page chair's card, or the Decision Record skill to write up the decision once the panel has made it.

## Output format

**Debrief agenda: [role], [date and time]**
Chair: [role]. Panel, in speaking order: [most junior role], [role], [hiring manager]. Decision recorded by: [role]. Candidates: [letters]. Vacancies: [number]. Scoring guide: [name and version].

**Before the meeting**
- Scores and evidence for every criterion submitted by [time], then locked.
- No discussion of candidates between interviews or before the meeting.
- Chair confirms all scores are in: [yes, or who is missing].

**Ground rules** (the chair reads these out): four or five lines, including the stop list.

**Agenda**

| Time | Item | Led by | What comes out of it |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [time] | Opening and ground rules | Chair | Rules read out |
| [time] | Criterion 1: [name], all candidates | Chair; speaking order as above | Scores, evidence, any changes with reasons |
| [time] | One row for each further criterion | Chair | As above |
| [time] | Decision | Panel | Decision and reasons for each candidate |
| [time] | Actions and close | Chair | Owners and dates |

**Record for each criterion**

| Candidate | Scores (by role) | Evidence cited | Changes and reasons |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |

**Bias watch**

| Bias | What it sounds like | What the chair says |
| --- | --- | --- |

**Decision**

| Candidate | Decision | Reasons by criterion | Dissent |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |

Recorded by [role] on [date]. Present: [roles].

One page, plus the record tables.

## Quality checks

- Every panel member submits scores and written evidence for every criterion before the meeting, and no item before or in place of the criterion items invites open discussion, first impressions or an "overall feel".
- Every criterion in the scoring guide has its own item, in the guide's order, and every item's speaking order starts with the most junior panel member and ends with the most senior.
- Every candidate is discussed on the same kind of evidence: nothing from outside the interview is used for some candidates and not others.
- The ground rules name halo, similarity and first-impression bias, each with what the chair does about it, and the stop list includes "culture fit".
- The decision item records, for each candidate, the panel's decision, the reasons by criterion and any dissent, written down on the day by a named role.
- Any score changed in the meeting has the change and the reason recorded.
- Timings add up to the meeting length, and nothing Claude wrote totals, ranks or recommends a candidate.

## Guardrails

- The one rule for this pack: Claude drafts the questions, guides and letters. Interviewers gather the evidence, and people make every hiring decision. This agenda structures the panel's discussion. Claude never scores, ranks or picks a candidate; if asked who should get the job, say that's the panel's decision and point back to the criteria.
- Personal data: candidates appear as letters, panel members as roles. Debrief notes and scores are personal data under UK GDPR and the Data Protection Act 2018, and a candidate can ask for them with a subject access request, which must be answered within one month. Speak and write as if the candidate will read it. Follow your organisation's AI policy before pasting scores in.
- Fairness: the same criteria, evidence and speaking order for every candidate, internal or external. Never infer or mention a protected characteristic, and stop any remark that touches one. Adjustments made for a candidate are part of a fair process, and the panel scores against the same guide whatever adjustments were made.
- Law: the Equality Act 2010 (direct and indirect discrimination; positive action under sections 158 and 159, where the section 159 tie-break applies only between candidates as qualified as each other and never as a blanket policy), the EHRC Employment Statutory Code of Practice, UK GDPR and the Data Protection Act 2018, and ICO guidance on keeping recruitment records. Northern Ireland has its own equality law. Check the current versions. This is a draft agenda, never legal advice.
- No invented facts: criteria, scores, panel roles and dates come from the user or stay as placeholders.
