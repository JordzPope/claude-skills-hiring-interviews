# Hiring Manager Intake

Produces the agenda and questions for a scoping meeting with the hiring manager for one vacancy, and the intake record written up afterwards. Or checks intake notes or a hiring brief someone already has. It gets the hiring manager to say what this hire must do in year one, and what the job can do without, before anything reaches a candidate.

## When to use

- "I've got a kick-off call tomorrow for four team leader roles. What should I ask the manager?"
- "Write up my notes from this intake call so the hiring manager can sign them off."
- "The manager just wants 'someone good'. How do I get them to be specific?"
- "Check this hiring brief before we open the vacancy."
- "We've never hired for this role before. Help me run the scoping meeting."

Wrong tool for turning the agreed outcomes into interview criteria (use Role Success Profile next), for testing essential criteria for fairness (Essential Criteria Review), or for anything about a particular candidate.

## Inputs

Ask once for anything missing. Defaults in brackets.

1. **Essential:** the vacancy: job title, how many roles, the team, and what prompted the hire (growth, a leaver, a new service). A job description or the manager's email can be pasted in.
2. **Essential:** what's needed now: questions for a meeting that hasn't happened yet, a write-up of notes from one that has, or a check of a brief. [questions for the meeting, plus a blank intake record]
3. Who's in the meeting and how long it runs. [the hiring manager and the TA partner, 30 minutes, by video]
4. Fixed points already known: pay range or band, location and hours, start date, whether internal candidates can apply. [all to confirm in the meeting]
5. Your hiring process and any policy on internal applicants or direct appointments. [two interview stages, open to internal and external candidates on the same terms]

Never ask for the names of possible candidates or the person leaving, or for anyone's health, absence record, protected characteristics or contact details. The intake is about the job. If notes name a person, replace the name with their role and say you've done so.

## Steps

1. Read what the user has given, including any job description, email or notes. If they've given existing intake notes or a hiring brief, this is a check job: run the Quality checks on it, list every problem with where it sits and why it matters, then write a corrected intake record.
2. Ask for missing essentials in one message, five questions at most, with the defaults shown. If the user says "just draft it", use the defaults and list them as assumptions.
3. Build the agenda in this order, because each part rests on the one before: why the role exists now; what the person must have achieved by the end of year one; what the work involves week to week; must-haves and nice-to-haves; the process, dates and panel; pay and practical facts; who does what next. Give each item minutes that add up to the meeting length.
4. Write two to four open questions for each agenda item, the kind that make the manager describe real work and results:
   - "Twelve months from now, what will this person have done that tells you the hire worked?"
   - "Think of the best person you've seen in this job. What did they do that others didn't?"
   - "What does a hard week in this job look like?"
   - "What could a new starter learn in the first three months with normal training?"
   - For every must-have: "If someone had everything else on this list and lacked this, could they do the job?"

   Add a follow-up for vague answers. When the manager says "rockstar", "good culture fit", "natural leader" or "hits the ground running", ask "What would I see them doing that shows that?" and write down the behaviour they describe.
5. After the meeting, write the intake record from the user's notes. Make each year-one outcome a result someone could check at twelve months, with a measure, a standard or a placeholder for the manager to set ("team's first-contact resolution at [target] by [month]"). A must-have is something the job can't be done without on day one, even with induction and training; everything else is a nice-to-have. Move anything that fails that test and say why. Replace length-of-service asks ("five years in a similar role") with the capability those years were meant to show.
6. Sweep the notes for the problems a hiring manager rarely sees in their own brief:
   - **Vague asks** (rockstar, ninja, the right attitude, culture fit): rewrite each as the behaviour meant, or list it as an open question for the manager. The words "culture fit" never appear in the record as a criterion.
   - **Must-haves the job doesn't need**, especially any that could shut out a group with a protected characteristic: a degree, a driving licence, years of experience, fixed hours, "native English". Flag them for Essential Criteria Review.
   - **Health and absence asks.** Section 60 of the Equality Act 2010 limits questions about health and disability before a job offer, and questions about sickness absence count. Remove any such must-have or question. Using internal candidates' absence records as a criterion also risks disability discrimination.
   - **A decision already made about a person**: "it's theirs really", "the interviews are a formality", a favourite who skips a stage. Record that every candidate, internal or external, goes through the same stages against the same criteria, and that any panel member who manages or knows a candidate says so at the start. If the organisation has decided to appoint someone directly, tell the user plainly that interviewing for a filled job wastes candidates' time and damages trust, and point to the policy on direct appointments.
   - **A request to hire from a particular group.** Positive action under sections 158 and 159 is narrow: encouraging applications from an under-represented group and, between candidates as qualified as each other, a tie-break. It never allows a quota or a criterion based on a protected characteristic. Refer the request to HR.
7. Write the process section: each stage with its format, length and dates; the panel for each interview by role, two people at least; how candidates ask for adjustments; when candidates hear back after each stage. Leave any date the manager didn't give as a placeholder.
8. Run the Quality checks below and fix what fails before showing anything.
9. Present the agenda and questions, or the intake record, then **Assumptions** and **Check before you use it**: the pay range against your pay structure, the dates against the panel's diaries, any must-have the manager hasn't confirmed, and your policy on internal applicants.
10. Offer a .docx, a short email asking the hiring manager to confirm the record, or Role Success Profile to turn the year-one outcomes into four to six criteria.

## Output format

**Before the meeting: intake agenda and questions, [job title]**
[Hiring manager role] and [TA role], [length], [video or room], [date].

| Minutes | Item | Questions |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [start to end] | [agenda item] | [two to four questions, with a follow-up for vague answers] |

**After the meeting: intake record, [job title], [number] roles**
Agreed by [hiring manager role] and [TA role] on [date]. Status: [draft for the hiring manager to confirm].

1. **Why the role exists now**: [one or two sentences]
2. **Year-one outcomes**

| Outcome | How we'll know | By when |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [result] | [measure, standard or placeholder] | [month] |

3. **What the work involves**: [five to eight main tasks]
4. **Must-haves**

| Must-have | Why the job needs it on day one |
| --- | --- |

5. **Nice-to-haves**: [list]
6. **What the job doesn't need**: [anything discussed and ruled out, with the reason]
7. **Process and dates**

| Stage | Format and length | Panel (roles) | Dates |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |

Adjustments: [how candidates ask]. Candidates hear back within [number] working days of each stage. Every candidate goes through the same stages.

8. **Pay and practical facts**: [pay range], [location], [hours and pattern], [contract type], [start date].
9. **Open questions**: [each with the role who'll answer it and by when]

The agenda fits on one page; the record runs to two pages at most.

## Quality checks

- Every year-one outcome is a result someone could check at twelve months, with a measure, a standard or a marked placeholder.
- Every must-have has a reason the job needs it on day one; anything a new starter could learn through normal induction and training sits under nice-to-haves.
- No vague ask (rockstar, culture fit, the right attitude, natural leader) is left in the record; each is rewritten as a behaviour or listed as an open question.
- No must-have or question touches health, sickness absence, disability or any other protected characteristic.
- Nothing says or suggests a candidate is already chosen, and internal and external candidates have the same stages and the same criteria.
- The process section names every stage with a date or a marked placeholder, a panel of two or more roles for each interview, how candidates ask for adjustments and when they hear back.
- The pay range is a figure from the user or a marked placeholder.

## Guardrails

- The one rule for this pack: "Claude drafts the questions, guides and letters. Interviewers gather the evidence, and people make every hiring decision." The intake record says what the job needs. Who gets the job is for the panel to decide, on evidence from the same process for everyone.
- Personal data: work with roles. Intake notes that mention a named person are personal data under UK GDPR and the Data Protection Act 2018, and that person can ask to see them through a subject access request. Keep people out of the record, and follow your organisation's AI policy before pasting anything in.
- Fairness: the same criteria and the same process for every candidate. Never infer or mention protected characteristics, and rewrite wording that is age-coded, gendered or tied to nationality.
- Law: in England, Scotland and Wales this draws on the Equality Act 2010 (direct and indirect discrimination; reasonable adjustments, section 20; health questions before an offer, section 60; positive action, sections 158 and 159) and the EHRC Employment Statutory Code of Practice. Northern Ireland has its own equality law. Check the current versions on legislation.gov.uk and the EHRC website, and your own policies. This is a draft, never legal advice.
- No invented facts: pay, dates, numbers and outcomes come from the user or stay as placeholders.
