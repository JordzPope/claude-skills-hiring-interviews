# Interview Invitation

Produces the invitation a candidate gets for an interview or assessment: when and where, how long, who they'll meet, what it covers, what to bring and how to ask for adjustments. Or checks an invitation someone has already written. Candidates who know what's coming can book the time off, find the room and prepare, and one plain adjustments line lets anyone ask for what would help without explaining why.

## When to use

- "Write the invite for the second-stage practical and interview for the engineer roles."
- "I need an interview confirmation we can load into the ATS for the Customer Adviser group sessions."
- "Check this invitation before the coordinator sends it to the shortlist."
- "Candidates keep going to main reception and missing their slot. Sort out our invite."
- "What should an interview invite say about adjustments?"

Wrong tool for the questions you share ahead (use Advance Questions Sheet), for answering a candidate who asks for an adjustment (use Adjustment Request Reply) or for writing your rules on candidates' use of AI (use Candidate AI Use Statement). The invitation points to all three.

## Inputs

Ask once for anything missing. Defaults in brackets.

1. **Essential:** the role, the stage (first interview, second interview, assessment day) and the format: video, phone or in person. [one structured interview by video]
2. **Essential:** the date, start time and length, or the slots candidates choose from, and the day the invitation goes out. [placeholders; sent at least five working days before the interview]
3. For video: the platform, the link, how to test it and a number to call if it fails. For in person: the full address, the entrance, where to report, parking or transport, and step-free access. [placeholders, listed under Check before you use it]
4. Who's on the panel by role, and what the interview covers: the kinds of question, any task, practical or presentation, and whether notes are allowed. [the hiring manager and a talent acquisition partner; questions on the role's criteria; notes allowed]
5. What candidates need to bring, and whether your process checks right to work at this stage. [nothing to bring; right to work is checked after an offer]
6. Who handles adjustment requests and by when, and anything sent with the invitation: questions in advance, an AI use statement, the candidate privacy notice. [the recruitment coordinator, three working days before the interview; the privacy notice link only]

Never ask for candidates' names, contact details, health information or protected characteristics. The draft keeps [first name] for the ATS or the coordinator to fill in.

## Steps

Numbers in square brackets in these steps and the Quality checks are defaults. Use the user's own figure if they give one; otherwise use the default without asking.

1. Read what the user has given, including any interview plan, job description or ATS template they attach. If they've given an existing invitation, this is a check job: run the Quality checks on it, list every problem with where it sits and why it matters, then write a corrected invitation.
2. Ask for missing essentials in one message, five questions at most, showing the defaults. If they say "just draft it", use the defaults, leave the date, link and address as placeholders, and list the assumptions.
3. Check the notice. Count working days from the day the invitation reaches the candidate to the interview: at least [five], so candidates in work can book time off, arrange childcare and travel, and ask for adjustments early enough to set them up. If the dates give less, say so at the top of your reply and suggest later slots or a choice of slots. Check every weekday against its date.
4. Put the practical details first, in short labelled lines: day and date, start time and length or finish time, and format. For video, give the platform, the link, how to test it beforehand, whether cameras are expected and a phone number to call if the link fails. For in person, give the full address, which entrance to use and where to report, parking or public transport, and step-free access. If the user hasn't told you the venue's access, ask, or list it under Check before you use it.
5. Name the panel by role ("a field supervisor and a talent acquisition partner"). If your practice is to give interviewers' names, add them when you send it, outside the chat.
6. Describe what the interview covers in plain words: how many main questions and what kind (examples from your experience, what-would-you-do scenarios), any task, practical or presentation and how long each part takes, whether notes are allowed, and that everyone at this stage is asked the same questions. If you share questions or themes in advance, say when they'll arrive. Leave the questions themselves and anything from the scoring guide out of the invitation.
7. List only what the stage needs candidates to bring: notes, a portfolio, sturdy shoes for a practical, glasses for a reading task. Ask for right to work documents only where your process checks them at this stage, the same for every candidate, and point to the Home Office guidance on acceptable documents and online checks. Asking for "your passport" alone shuts out people who prove their right to work another valid way.
8. Write the adjustments line as one plain sentence with a contact and a date: "If there's anything that would make the interview easier for you, tell [role] by [date] and we'll arrange it. You don't need to tell us why." Ask nothing about health, disability, a condition, medication or sickness absence, in the invitation or in any form it links to. Section 60 of the Equality Act 2010 limits health and disability questions before a job offer to a few set purposes, such as making adjustments to the interview, and the adjustments line covers that purpose without asking any. Rewrite any line that asks candidates to "declare" or "list" conditions.
9. If there's a Candidate AI Use Statement, add one line and the link: preparing is fine, answers in the interview must be the candidate's own, and agreed accessibility tools are always fine. If there isn't one and the interview is remote or technical, offer the Candidate AI Use Statement skill.
10. Write in plain English at about reading age 12, with "you" and "we" and short sentences. Spell out or replace every acronym and internal term: stage codes, assessment shorthand, job-title initials, team nicknames. Aim for under [300] words before links. End with a contact role for questions and how to rearrange.
11. Run the Quality checks and fix what fails.
12. Present the invitation, then **Assumptions** and **Check before you use it**: each date, weekday and time; the link or address, entrance and access details; the panel roles; whether right to work is checked at this stage; the adjustments contact and date.
13. Offer a .docx, a text message reminder under 300 characters, an ATS template with merge fields, or the Advance Questions Sheet, Adjustment Request Reply or Candidate AI Use Statement skill.

## Output format

**Subject:** Your interview for [role]: [weekday date], [start time]

Hi [first name],

Thank you for applying for [role]. We'd like to invite you to [stage, in plain words].

**When:** [weekday date], [start time] to [finish time] (about [length])
**Where:** [Video: platform, link, "test your link here: [test link]", "if the link doesn't work, call [number]"] or [In person: full address, entrance, where to report, parking or transport, step-free access]
**Who you'll meet:** [panel roles]
**What happens:** [number] questions about [themes], [any task, practical or presentation, and how long each part takes]. Everyone at this stage is asked the same questions. [You're welcome to bring notes.] [We'll send you the main questions on [date].]
**What to bring:** [items, or "Nothing, just yourself."] [Right to work line only if checked at this stage]
**Anything that would help you take part?** Tell [role] by [date] at [contact]. You don't need to tell us why.
**Using AI:** [one line and a link to your Candidate AI Use Statement, or remove]
**Your information:** [link to the candidate privacy notice]
**Questions, or need to rearrange?** Contact [role] at [contact].

[Sender name], [role]

Under [300] words before links. No tables, so it reads well on a phone.

## Quality checks

- The invitation gives the day and date, start time, length or finish time, and format; every weekday matches its date; and it reaches the candidate at least [five] working days before the interview.
- A video invitation has the link, a way to test it and a number to call if it fails. An in-person invitation has the full address, the entrance and where to report, how to get there, and the venue's step-free access.
- The panel appears by role, and what the interview covers (question types, any task or practical, timings, whether notes are allowed) is clear to someone who has never worked there.
- The adjustments line names a contact and a date and asks what would help. No line asks about health, disability, a condition, medication or sickness absence.
- Right to work documents are asked for only if the process checks them at this stage, and then by pointing to the Home Office's acceptable documents and online checks.
- Every acronym and internal term is spelled out or replaced, and the invitation is under [300] words before links.
- No candidate names, health details or protected characteristics appear in the draft, and the greeting stays [first name].

## Guardrails

- The one rule for this pack: Claude drafts the questions, guides and letters. Interviewers gather the evidence, and people make every hiring decision. This invitation gets every candidate at the stage to the interview with the same information. The panel gathers the evidence on the day, and people decide.
- Personal data: draft one invitation for everyone at the stage and keep candidates' names out of the chat; the ATS or the coordinator adds them. Never ask for health information. If a candidate replies with health details, keep them out of Claude and use the Adjustment Request Reply skill with the condition removed. Follow your organisation's AI policy.
- Fairness: every candidate at the stage gets the same invitation, the same notice, the same description of what's covered and the same route to adjustments. Never infer or mention protected characteristics. Check the wording for assumptions about who drives, who has a laptop at home or who can take a morning off at short notice, and offer a choice of slots where you can.
- Law: section 20 of the Equality Act 2010 (reasonable adjustments, including to the interview), section 60 (health and disability questions before an offer) and the EHRC Employment Code; UK GDPR and the Data Protection Act 2018 (link the candidate privacy notice and say if any tool is used on candidates' information); the Home Office guidance on right to work checks. Check the current versions. This is a draft, never legal advice.
- No invented facts: dates, times, addresses, links, access details and panel roles come from the user or stay as placeholders.
