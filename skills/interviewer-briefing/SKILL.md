# Interviewer Briefing

Produces the one page a panel reads before interview day: who does what, the timetable, which guides to use, what never to ask, the adjustments agreed for each candidate, how scoring works and where the notes go. Or checks a briefing already written. Every panel member then runs the interview the same way, including the senior colleague who sits on one panel a year.

## When to use

- "Write a one-page brief for the finance business partner panel next Tuesday."
- "One of our directors is on the panel and hasn't interviewed here before. What do they need to know?"
- "Turn this interview plan into a briefing email for the hiring managers."
- "What should I tell the panel about the adjustments we've agreed?"
- "Check my panel briefing before I send it."

Wrong tool for training interviewers (use the Interviewer Practice Session), writing the questions (use the Structured Interview Guide) or replying to a candidate about an adjustment (use the Adjustment Request Reply). For the meeting after the interviews, use the Debrief Agenda.

## Inputs

Ask once for anything missing. Defaults in brackets.

1. **Essential:** the role, stage, date, place and format, and the panel by role. [the hiring manager chairs, a second panel member asks some of the questions, the TA partner takes the main notes]
2. **Essential:** the schedule: how many candidates, the slot length and the sections of each interview. [45-minute interviews, with 15 minutes after each one to finish notes and score]
3. The interview guide, scoring guide and note sheet, or where they're kept. [sent separately; placeholders if not yet written]
4. Adjustments agreed for each candidate, written as what the panel must do. [none agreed yet; the TA partner confirms before the day]
5. Where notes and scores go, and by when. [the applicant tracking system (ATS) within one working day; paper copies shredded once uploaded]

Never ask for candidates' names, diagnoses, health details or protected characteristics. For adjustments the panel needs only what to do ("show each question on screen as well as reading it out"), never why. If the user includes a diagnosis or condition, leave it out of the briefing and say so.

## Steps

Numbers in square brackets in these steps and the Quality checks are defaults. Use the user's own figure if they give one; otherwise use the default without asking.

1. Read what the user has given, including any attached interview plan, guide or schedule. If they've given an existing briefing, panel pack or briefing email, this is a check job: run the Quality checks on it, list every problem with where it sits and why it matters, then write a corrected briefing.
2. Ask for missing essentials in one message, five questions at most, with the defaults shown. If the user says "just draft it", use the defaults and list them as assumptions.
3. **Who does what.** By role: the chair (welcomes the candidate, introduces the panel, explains the format and any adjustment, keeps time, invites the candidate's questions, closes), who asks which questions, and who takes the main notes. Say every panel member also writes their own evidence notes, because each one scores alone.
4. **Timings.** Build a timetable: a pre-brief of [15] minutes before the first candidate, each interview with its sections in minutes, [15] minutes after each interview to finish notes and score, and the debrief. Check each interview's sections add up to its slot and nothing runs into the next candidate. For a video interview, say who sends the link and what happens if the connection drops.
5. **What you'll use.** Name the interview guide, scoring guide and note sheet and where each one is. Give the rules from Campion, Palmer and Campion's (1997) components of structure in three lines: the same questions in the same order for every candidate, probes from the guide only, notes taken during the interview.
6. **Never ask.** A short list:
   - health, disability, sickness absence or medication before an offer (Equality Act 2010, section 60); [the TA partner] handles the narrow exceptions, such as arranging adjustments for the interview;
   - age or date of birth, marriage or civil partnership, pregnancy, plans for children, childcare or other caring;
   - religion or belief, sexual orientation, gender reassignment, race, nationality or where someone is "originally from";
   - immigration status: [the recruitment coordinator] carries out right to work checks outside the interview, the same way for every candidate, following Home Office guidance;
   - trade union membership.
   Where the job has a genuine requirement (a shift pattern, driving, travel), write one factual question about the requirement for the panel to ask every candidate in the same words. If a candidate volunteers personal information, the panel doesn't follow it up or write it down.
7. **Adjustments.** For each candidate who has one, by letter: what the panel does. Nothing about why. Add that using an adjustment never affects a score.
8. **Scoring and the debrief.** Score each answer alone against the scoring guide, straight after the interview, before any discussion. Nobody discusses candidates between interviews, including in the gaps. Scores go to [the TA partner] before the debrief. At the debrief all scores are shown at once before anyone gives a view, then the panel goes criterion by criterion. The most senior person on the panel follows the same order as everyone else.
9. **Notes and confidentiality.** Notes and scores are personal data, and the candidate can request them, so write only what you'd be content for them to read. Upload to [the ATS] within [one working day] and shred paper copies. Keep no copies on personal devices, in email or in chat channels, and share candidate details with the panel and the TA team only. Don't paste candidate details into AI tools outside [the organisation's approved tools].
10. Fit it on one page. Point to the guides rather than copying them in. If it runs over, cut explanation first and keep the rules.
11. Run the Quality checks below and fix what fails before showing anything.
12. Present the briefing, then **Assumptions** and **Check before you use it**: the timings and rooms, that each candidate has agreed what the panel is told about their adjustment, where notes go, and your retention period.
13. Offer a .docx, the briefing as an email, the Interviewer Note Sheet for the panel, or the Debrief Agenda for the meeting afterwards.

## Output format

**Interviewer briefing: [role], [stage], [date]**
[place or video platform] · Contact on the day: [TA partner role]

1. **Who does what**

   | Panel member (role) | On the day |
   | --- | --- |

2. **Timings**

   | Time | What |
   | --- | --- |

   Each interview: [sections and minutes, e.g. welcome 5, questions 30, candidate's questions 5, close 5].
3. **What you'll use**: guide, scoring guide and note sheet, where they are, and the structure rules in three lines.
4. **Never ask**: the list from step 6, plus any requirement question to ask everyone.
5. **Adjustments**

   | Candidate | What the panel does |
   | --- | --- |

6. **Scoring and the debrief**: four lines.
7. **Notes and confidentiality**: four lines.

One page.

## Quality checks

- A chair and a main note-taker are named by role, every question has an owner, and every panel member is told to write their own notes.
- Each interview's sections add up to its slot, and the timetable leaves time after every interview to finish notes and score before the next candidate.
- The never-ask list covers health, disability and sickness absence before an offer (section 60) and the protected characteristics, and nothing elsewhere in the briefing invites any of them.
- Adjustments say only what the panel does; no diagnosis, condition, medication or other health detail appears.
- Each interviewer scores alone against the scoring guide before any discussion, nobody discusses candidates between interviews, and no panel member's view is heard before all the scores are in.
- Notes and scores go only to the place named, by a stated time, and never to a wider group; the briefing says they're personal data a candidate can request.
- It fits on one page, and candidates appear as letters.

## Guardrails

- The one rule for this pack: Claude drafts the questions, guides and letters. Interviewers gather the evidence, and people make every hiring decision. Claude drafts the briefing; the panel runs the interviews, each member scores alone, and the panel makes the decision at the debrief.
- Work with roles and candidate letters. Share only the adjustment the panel must make, never the reason, and only with the candidate's agreement on what's shared. Interview notes and scores are personal data under UK GDPR and the Data Protection Act 2018; follow the ICO's guidance on recruitment records, your retention policy and your organisation's AI policy.
- Brief every panel the same way and give every candidate the same questions. Never infer or mention protected characteristics. The Equality Act 2010 covers direct and indirect discrimination at interview, restricts pre-offer health questions (section 60) and requires reasonable adjustments for disabled candidates (section 20). Right to work checks follow Home Office guidance and are done the same way for everyone.
- Check the current versions of the Equality Act 2010, the EHRC Employment Code, the ICO's guidance and the Home Office right to work guidance, and your own policy. This is a draft briefing, never legal advice.
- Dates, rooms, timings and adjustments come from the user or stay as placeholders.
