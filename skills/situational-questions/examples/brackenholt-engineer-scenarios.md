# Smart-Meter Engineer: scenario questions (draft)

From: field supervisor, Yorkshire patch
For: final interviews at the Sheffield training centre, on the same day as the practical assessment. Candidates are a mix of qualified engineers from other suppliers and people who've just finished an apprenticeship.
Time: about five minutes per question. Each setup is read out and shown on a card.
Source: all six are based on things that have happened on the Yorkshire patch in the last year.

Criteria for these questions:

1. **Puts safety first:** stops when something isn't safe, follows the procedure, makes the site safe.
2. **Looks after the customer:** explains what's happening, keeps them informed, notices when someone needs extra help.
3. **Manages a day of jobs:** plans the day, tells the planners early when things slip, makes sensible calls on what comes first.

## S1. Puts safety first

Setup: "You arrive to swap an old electricity meter for a smart meter. The customer works nights and wants it done quickly so they can sleep. When you take the cover off, you see scorch marks around the meter and on the cable going into it. The customer says it's always looked like that and asks you to get on with it. What would you do?"

Probes: "What would you do first?" "What would you say to the customer?" "The customer says they'll complain if you leave without fitting the meter. What now?"

A strong answer weighs up:

- stops work and touches nothing further until it's safe
- follows the procedure for damaged equipment [procedure to confirm with the field safety lead]
- explains calmly and clearly why the job can't go ahead today
- checks whether the customer still has a safe supply and whether anyone in the home depends on it
- records and reports what they found, and makes sure a follow-up is booked

## S2. Looks after the customer

Setup: "It's your second job of the day. The customer has taken unpaid leave to be in, and the last two appointments were missed. The job turns out to be more complicated than the booking said and will take an extra hour. Your next customer relies on electricity for medical equipment and is expecting you at 1pm. What would you do?"

Probes: "Who would you contact first, and what would you say?" "What would you say to the customer you're with?" "The planner says no one else can cover the 1pm job. What now?"

A strong answer weighs up:

- tells the planners straight away, before either customer is let down
- is honest with the current customer about the extra time and what happens next
- treats the 1pm customer's medical need as a reason they may need to come first, or at least get a call
- doesn't rush the current job in a way that cuts safety corners
- agrees a plan with the planner and keeps both customers informed

## S3. Puts safety first

Setup: "A customer asks you to skip the safety checks at the end of the job so you can finish quicker. Would you do it?"

Probes: "Why?" "What would you say to them?" "What if they kept asking?"

A strong answer weighs up:

- says no
- explains why the checks matter for the customer's safety
- stays polite and finishes the checks

## S4. Manages a day of jobs

Setup: "You're on your third job and JobPad shows an Amber on your next address. You'd normally ring the Hub, but the Hub line is down. What would you do?"

Probes: "What would you do first?" "Who else could you contact?" "You still can't get through by the time you arrive. What then?"

A strong answer weighs up:

- knows what an Amber means and the Amber steps
- tries the backup route to the Hub through the patch supervisor
- doesn't start the job until the Amber has been checked
- records what happened in JobPad

## S5. Looks after the customer

Setup: "You've just fitted a smart meter, and the customer seems unsure how the in-home display works. They say someone in the family usually sorts this kind of thing out, but they're away this week. You're already 20 minutes late for your next job. What would you do?"

Probes: "What would you do before you leave?" "What would you tell the planner?" "The customer asks you to come back next week to show the rest of the family. What now?"

A strong answer: depends on the person. Use your experience here; you'll know a good answer when you hear it.

## S6. Culture fit

Setup: "The team goes to the pub after the monthly depot meeting. A newer engineer always heads straight home, and the others have started to notice. What would you do?"

Probes: "Would you say anything to them?" "What would you say to the rest of the team?" "What if they still didn't come along?"

A strong answer weighs up:

- makes the newer engineer feel part of the team and gets them along
- understands that a lot gets sorted out over a drink after the depot meeting
- backs up the rest of the team
