# Structured Interview Guide

Produces the guide every interviewer on a role works from: a short opening, the same scored questions in the same order for every candidate, set probes and timings, the candidate's own questions and the close. Or checks a guide someone already uses. It turns a role's criteria into one interview that every hiring manager runs the same way.

## When to use

- "Write a 45-minute interview guide for the team leader role from these criteria."
- "Our hiring managers all ask different things. Give me one guide they can all use."
- "I need competency questions with STAR probes for a first interview by video."
- "Check this interview script before the panel uses it next week."
- "We use Success Profiles. Build the interview from these behaviours and strengths."

Wrong tool for the rating scale behind each question (use the Scoring Guide), for deciding which criterion is tested at which stage (use the Interview Plan) or for building what-would-you-do questions in depth (use Situational Questions).

## Inputs

Ask once for anything missing. Defaults in brackets.

1. **Essential:** the role and the criteria this interview tests, ideally from the role success profile or the interview plan. [four to six criteria proposed by Claude from the job description, marked for you to confirm]
2. **Essential:** the stage, format and length of the slot. [first interview, by video, 45 minutes]
3. Who interviews, by role. [the hiring manager and a talent acquisition partner]
4. The mix of question types. [past-behaviour (STAR) questions for most criteria and one situational question; strengths questions as well if you use Success Profiles]
5. Who the candidates are likely to be: new to this kind of work, experienced, or internal and external together. [a mix of experience]

Never ask for candidate names, CVs or anything about a particular candidate. The guide is the same for everyone, so it never needs them.

## Steps

Numbers in square brackets in these steps and the Quality checks are defaults. Use the user's own figure if they give one; otherwise use the default without asking.

1. Read what the user has given, including any attached job description, success profile, interview plan or existing guide. If they've given an existing guide, this is a check job: run the Quality checks on it, list every problem with the question number or section where it sits and why it matters, then write a corrected guide that keeps their criteria and their good questions. If the draft calls the candidate he or she, switch to "the candidate" or "they" and say so.
2. Ask for missing essentials in one message, five questions at most, with the defaults shown. If the user says "just draft it", proceed on the defaults and list them as assumptions.
3. Fix the criteria first. List the criteria this interview tests, four to six at most, each written as a behaviour someone could see in the job. If a criterion is "culture fit", "attitude" or "personality", ask what behaviour it stands for (works through a busy queue without cutting corners, asks for help early) and use that instead. Criteria the Interview Plan tests at another stage stay out of this guide.
4. Apply the content side of structure from Campion, Palmer and Campion (1997): questions based on the job's criteria, the same questions for every candidate, limited and planned prompting, better question types, and the candidate's own questions held until the end. Sackett and colleagues' 2022 re-analysis ranked structured interviews top among selection methods for predicting job performance, and these features are what make an interview structured.
5. Write the questions: one main question per criterion, and a second for the one or two criteria that matter most if the time allows.
   - **Past behaviour (STAR):** "Tell me about a time you..." asking for one real example. For roles open to people new to the work, say examples can come from any part of life: a previous job, study, caring, volunteering, sport.
   - **Situational:** a short, realistic dilemma from the job, written out in full in advance, with the same facts for everyone. Use the Situational Questions skill for these where there's time.
   - **Strengths** (Success Profiles users): short questions about what the candidate enjoys and does well, each tied to a named strength in the role's profile. Label every criterion with its Success Profiles element (behaviour, strength, experience, ability or technical), and use the scale your organisation sets.
   Every question is open, asks one thing, uses plain words a candidate from outside the organisation would understand, and points to no particular answer.
6. Write two or three set probes per question. STAR probes get at each part of the example: "What was your part in it?", "What did you do next?", "What happened in the end?", "Looking back, what would you change?". Probes are the same for every candidate. Interviewers may repeat or rephrase a question and ask nothing else.
7. Set the timings. Allow [three] minutes for a rapport opening (introductions, how the interview runs, that notes are taken, a check that any agreed adjustments are in place). Allow about [seven] minutes per past-behaviour or situational question with its probes, or [five] for a role new to the work and for strengths questions. Then [five] minutes for the candidate's questions and [two] for the close. Add it all up. The total must leave time spare: at least [five] minutes in a slot of 45 minutes or more, [three] in a shorter one. If it doesn't fit, cut the second question on the least important criterion first, then trim the opening to [two] minutes and the candidate's questions to [three], then the time per question to no less than [four]. Say what you cut. If every criterion still can't get a question, say so and suggest a longer slot, or moving a criterion to another stage with the Interview Plan.
8. Write the close: thanks, what happens next and when the candidate will hear, with the date as a placeholder.
9. Add the interviewer notes: ask every question in the order written, use only the set probes, write down what the candidate said and did, score each answer against the Scoring Guide on your own before talking to anyone, and don't discuss candidates between interviews. If your organisation shares questions in advance, every candidate gets the same set at the same point (use the Advance Questions Sheet).
10. Run the Quality checks below and fix what fails before showing anything.
11. Present the guide, then **Assumptions** and **Check before you use it**: the criteria match the agreed success profile, the slot length is right, the close date is one you'll keep, and every interviewer on the role has the same version.
12. Offer a .docx, a one-page desk version for interviewers, the Scoring Guide for these questions, or a run through the Question Fairness Check.

## Output format

**Structured interview guide: [role], [stage]**
Format: [video or in person]. Slot: [minutes]. Interviewers: [roles]. Version: [date].

**Criteria tested in this interview**
| # | Criterion (as a behaviour) | Success Profiles element, if used | Questions |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |

**Running order**
| Section | Minutes | Running total |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Opening (not scored) | | |
| Q1 to Q[n] | | |
| Candidate's questions | | |
| Close | | |
| Spare | | [slot length] |

**Opening** (not scored, 60 words or fewer to read out): welcome; who's interviewing, by role; how long it takes; everyone gets the same questions; notes are taken; take your time and ask for a question to be repeated; check that any agreed adjustments are working.

**Questions** (asked in this order, to every candidate). For each:
**Q[n]. [criterion]**, [past behaviour, situational or strengths], [minutes]
Ask: "[question]"
Probes: "[probe]", "[probe]", "[probe]"
Notes: [space for what the candidate said and did]
Score against: Scoring Guide, criterion [n].

**Candidate's questions** (not scored): "What would you like to ask us?"

**Close**: thanks, next steps, "You'll hear from us by [date]."

**Interviewer notes**: the rules from step 9, five lines at most.

Two pages at most, excluding note space.

## Quality checks

- Every scored question maps to one criterion on the list, and every criterion has at least one question. No scored question tests "culture fit", a general impression or anything off the list.
- Every candidate gets the same questions in the same order: no alternative questions for different kinds of candidate, no optional extras and no room for interviewers to add their own.
- Hypothetical questions appear only as planned situational questions, written out in full and tied to a criterion.
- Every question asks one thing, is open and neutral, and has two or three set probes that are the same for everyone.
- The timings, counting the opening, the candidate's questions and the close, fit the slot with time spare: at least [five] minutes in a slot of 45 minutes or more, [three] in a shorter one.
- The guide opens with a short, unscored rapport opening and ends with the candidate's questions and a close that says when they'll hear.
- The interviewer notes say to record what the candidate said and did, and to score each answer against the Scoring Guide alone, before any discussion.

## Guardrails

- The one rule for this pack: Claude drafts the questions, guides and letters. Interviewers gather the evidence, and people make every hiring decision. This guide sets the questions. The interviewers ask them and score the answers, and the panel decides. Claude never scores or ranks a candidate's answers.
- Personal data: the blank guide holds no candidate details. Once an interviewer writes notes on it, it holds personal data under UK GDPR and the Data Protection Act 2018, so the candidate can ask to see it through a subject access request, and it's kept only as long as your retention policy and the ICO's guidance on recruitment records allow. Refer to candidates by letter, follow your organisation's AI policy, and never paste completed notes into Claude.
- Fairness: every candidate gets the same questions, probes, timings and scoring. Never infer or mention protected characteristics. Check the wording for jargon, idioms and references that favour insiders or one group, and run the final set through the Question Fairness Check.
- Law: the Equality Act 2010 applies in England, Scotland and Wales (Northern Ireland has its own equality law). Section 60 limits health and disability questions before an offer, so the guide asks none. Section 20 requires reasonable adjustments for disabled candidates, such as extra time, questions in writing or breaks; adjustments change how the interview runs, and every candidate is still assessed on the same criteria. The EHRC Employment Statutory Code of Practice explains both. Check the current versions. This is a draft, never legal advice.
- No invented facts: criteria, timings, dates and interviewer roles come from the user or stay as placeholders.
