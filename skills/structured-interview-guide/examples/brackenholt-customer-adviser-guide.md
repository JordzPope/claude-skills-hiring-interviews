# Customer Adviser: first interview guide (draft)

From: contact centre manager, Sheffield
For: team leaders running first interviews for the Customer Adviser roles
Format: video, 30-minute slot, one interviewer

## Criteria (from the Customer Adviser success profile)

1. **Listens and gets to the real problem:** lets the customer explain, checks they've understood, asks the right questions.
2. **Gets the detail right:** follows the steps, checks accounts and readings, records what was agreed.
3. **Stays calm under pressure:** keeps steady with an upset customer or a long queue.
4. **Owns the problem:** sees it through or hands it on properly, and tells the customer what happens next.

Many applicants haven't worked in an office before. For the "tell me about a time" questions, examples can come from any job, study, caring, volunteering or sport.

## Welcome (not scored)

"Hi, thanks for joining. I'm a team leader in our Sheffield contact centre. We've got about half an hour. I'll ask everyone the same questions, and I'll take notes so I remember what you said. Take your time, and ask me to repeat anything. Is everything you asked us to set up working for you?"

## Questions

**Q1. Listens and gets to the real problem** (tell me about a time)
"Tell me about a time someone came to you with a problem and it turned out to be different from what they first said."
Probes: "How did you find out what the real problem was?" "What did you do next?" "How did it end?"

**Q2. Gets the detail right** (tell me about a time)
"Tell me about a time you had to follow a set of steps exactly, at work or anywhere else."
Probes: "What made it tricky to get right?" "How did you check it was right?" "What happened?"
For candidates who've worked in a contact centre before, ask this instead: "How did you keep on top of your quality scores in your last job?"

**Q3. Stays calm under pressure** (what would you do)
"A customer calls because their bill has doubled. They're shouting, and you can see eight calls waiting. On screen, you can see the bill used an estimated meter reading. What would you do?"
Probes: "What would you say first?" "They still won't let you speak. What now?" "How would you get ready for the next call?"

**Q4. Owns the problem** (tell me about a time)
"Tell me about a time you promised to sort something out for someone and it took longer than you expected."
Probes: "What did you do to keep it moving?" "How did you keep them updated?" "What happened in the end?"

**Q5. Listens and gets to the real problem** (what would you do)
"A customer rings to say their smart meter display has stopped working. As you talk, they mention they've been putting the heating on less because they're worried about money. What would you do?"
Probes: "What would you ask them?" "What would you do about the display?" "What would you tell them before you end the call?"

**Q6. Stays calm under pressure** (tell me about a time)
"Tell me about a time you had a lot to do and not enough time to do it."
Probes: "How did you decide what to do first?" "How did you feel, and what did you do about it?" "What happened?"

**Q7. General**
"Where do you see yourself in five years?"
Probes: "Why that?" "What would help you get there?"

Interviewers: if you've got time left at the end, add a curveball scenario of your own to see how they think on their feet.

## Candidate's questions (not scored)

"What would you like to ask us about the job or about Brackenholt?"

## Close

"Thanks for your time today. We'll email you within five working days to let you know the outcome."

## After the interview

Score each answer from 1 to 4 on your own, using the Customer Adviser scoring guide, and put your scores in the ATS before the weekly team leader catch-up. Keep your notes to what the candidate said and did.
